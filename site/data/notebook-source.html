My everyday sourdough

Recorded 15 October 2026 · Based on Darren’s account

My simple baseline: mix, refrigerate for about 24 hours, then shape just before baking. Quantities stay flexible as I experiment.

Ingredients

One batch — 600 g total flour mixture, before adding starter
IngredientUsual amount
Rye flour50–100 g
Spelt flour100 g
Wheat gluten (77% protein product)80–100 g
White flour or lantveteEnough to bring the mixture to 600 g total
WaterAround 430 g
Starter200–250 g
Salt12–14 g

Mix and ferment

  1. Mix the flour mixture with the water.
  2. Add the starter and salt. Mix by hand or with a dough hook for 5–10 minutes.
  3. Put in a bowl and let rise in the fridge for around 24 hours.

Shape at the last minute

  1. Scrape the dough from the bowl onto baking paper with a cake scraper.
  2. Rub flour over the surface and shape it.
  3. Run a long knife over the top to create grooves, then bake.

Baking sequence

StageTemperatureFanTime
Start250°COn10 min
Continue220°COn10 min
Finish220°COff10 min

A long, thin loaf needs no extra time: 30 minutes total. A brick-shaped loaf gets another 5–10 minutes: 35–40 minutes total.

Experiments & observations
  • I experiment with time outside the fridge before or after refrigeration, herbs and other ingredients.
  • On a recent wetter batch, I used the baking paper to support the shape and held it together with a peg, because the dough would otherwise have flattened considerably. Reported 15 October 2026; baking date not recorded.

The peg observation is not a standard baking instruction; whether it went in the oven and what it was made of have not been recorded.

Maintaining my starter

Recorded 15 October 2026 · Darren’s personal observations and practice

Storage and feeding

  • Keep it cool in the fridge; don’t leave it unattended for more than a week.
  • When taken out for feeding, feed twice a day, doubling the total weight each time.
  • I think in quantities of 25 → 50 → 100 → 200 g. Add equal weights of flour and water: for example, 100 g starter + 50 g flour + 50 g water = 200 g.
  • If I’m not ready to bake, discard some to keep the quantity manageable.

Fragrance: what I look for

I smell the starter to check its fragrance. I want it aromatic, not vinegary or cheesy.

My stiff-starter refresh method

When I encounter a cheesy-smelling starter, I make a stiff starter, discard half and continue twice-daily feeding cycles, maintaining the drier consistency until the cheesy smell disappears.

Recorded as personal experience, not a validated rescue or food-safety procedure. A change in smell alone does not establish that a starter is safe.

Starting again from mature bread dough

I sometimes take a piece of dough that is ready to bake and use it to start the next starter. This dough already includes the flour mixture and salt from the loaf.
